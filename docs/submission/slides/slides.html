The problem

At the south pole, a ridge 673 m away decides whether a lander has power

  • Near the pole the Sun stays within a few degrees of the horizon. Small relief casts very long shadows.
  • A mission needs sunlight for power and the Earth in view for radio contact, at the same time, for days.
  • Both change hour by hour, differ between neighbouring sites, and depend on the height of the mast.

of sunlight lost in two years to this one ridge: 31 % of everything the site loses to terrain (observer at 2 m).

Our solution

Compare landing sites and dates on one screen

  • Compare two sites at any hour of a two-year window: Sun and Earth visible or hidden, time to the next rise or set.
  • Explain a loss: which terrain hides the Sun, how far away it is, how many hours it costs.
  • Plan: a 24-month calendar, landing windows for a stated requirement, days a landing can slip.
  • Change the mast height (0 to 20 m) and see what it buys.

20 published sites, from Apollo 11 to a point in an Artemis III candidate region. Any other coordinate: one command of the open-source core.

Real NASA data · scientific method

One science core computes every number; the views only read it

NASA and partner dataLOLA elevation: 5 m site models, 20 m and 60 m polar grids (LRO, NASA GSFC, PDS) · SLDEM2015 at 118 m (LOLA with Kaguya Terrain Camera data, JAXA) · SPICE kernels with DE440 (NASA/JPL NAIF) · lander coordinates from LROC
→
Science core (Python)360° terrain horizon traced on the sphere out to 300 km · Sun and Earth directions with light time and aberration · the Sun as a disc · rise and set events at six observer heights
→
Result packagesVersioned files with a SHA-256 for every array, the method and validation status of every number, and an uncertainty band on event times at the seven south-polar sites
→
ViewsWeb planner and Unreal Engine scene. They look values up and count down to published times. No science in the browser or the engine.
  • No raw data is stored in the repository: scripts download it and check every file against a committed checksum.
  • Every dataset, library and asset is listed with licence, source and retrieval time in CREDITS.md.
  • Reference data for checking: JPL Horizons, LOLA illumination maps (NASA GSFC), LROC images, Apollo and CLPS mission records.
  • Reproducible: python scripts/bootstrap.py --test sets up, downloads and runs the tests.

Both portals tried hands-on on 15 November 2026 (web/docs/tool_comparison). They find places; our tool compares dates and heights at a place. No accuracy ranking is claimed.

Limits

  • Event times on real terrain have no independent reference; one of two image checks fails.
  • Outside the polar grids the terrain is 118 m per pixel: nearby obstacles smaller than about 240 m are not seen.
  • Within 1 km of the site a result depends on single pixels and on a few metres of position.
  • The landing requirement shown is an example of the team, not a mission criterion. The sites are not official landing sites.

Use of AI: parts of the code and text were written with AI coding assistants and reviewed by the team (CREDITS.md). No image or video is AI-generated.
