Grey: terrain horizon at the selected observer height (horizon.elevation_deg). Markers: disc centres of the Sun and the Earth at the selected sample (timeline.series), without terrain.

360° panorama: azimuth left to right, elevation bottom to top. Grey: terrain horizon at the selected observer height. Lines: paths of the Sun and the Earth (disc centres, timeline.series) around the selected time, solid where the package window <body>_visible holds, faded where it does not. Drag to pan, use the wheel, a pinch or the buttons to zoom; point at a path to read its values, click or tap it to go to that time.

Loading the 3-D view…

Ground view from the site at real scale. The ground is the package's terrain patches (listed below this view, the finest where they overlap); beyond the largest, a curtain up to the package horizon. The coloured line is the package horizon profile, drawn on top as the reference, by the distance of the terrain that sets it (horizon.distance_m): < 1 km (near field) 1–10 km 10–100 km ≥ 100 km. The Sun and the Earth are at their package directions and true angular size; the light comes from the Sun. Shading and shadows come from the drawn surface and are for orientation only: whether the Sun or the Earth is visible is the package value in the overlay. Enhanced lighting (default) raises the exposure (more when the Sun is lower) and adds a soft fill light so that ground in shadow can be read; switch it off to see the Sun alone at plain exposure. Slope shading colours the drawn surface from light grey (flat) to red-brown (30° or steeper). Dashed outlines: the edge of each patch except the largest, in the colour named in the list of patches below; the flat dark grey beyond the largest patch is the package horizon, not drawn in relief. Dashed rings mark the Sun and the Earth at a fixed screen size and are not to scale; the disc inside each ring is drawn at its true angular size. Drag to look around, use the wheel or the buttons to zoom.

Loading the calendar…

Lunar days of timeline.calendar at the selected observer height, and the landing opportunities of timeline.opportunities for the chosen requirement, as the package gives them; nothing is recomputed in the browser.

Loading the Moon globe…

The Moon from a distance, in its body-fixed frame. It is lit along the Sun vector of the direction table (positions_km, from the centre of the Moon) at the selected time; the day/night boundary is the shading of that light. Red markers: the sites, at their latitude and longitude in the index and checked against site.position_km of the selected package (white, larger); click a marker or its label, or use Tab and Enter, to open its ground view at the same time. Markers on the far side are hidden. Orange and blue rings: where the Sun and Earth vectors meet the sphere (sub-solar and sub-Earth points), shown when they face you; the Sun and Earth tags at the edge of the disc point to each body in this view. Faint grid: latitude and longitude every 30°. Drag to turn, use the wheel or the buttons to zoom.

Not available in the packages, so not shown: the latitude and longitude of the sub-solar and sub-Earth points (the contract has no such fields; the markers follow the vectors only). Surface texture: NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, CGI Moon Kit (LROC WAC colour mosaic, polar gaps filled with LOLA albedo), for orientation only.
